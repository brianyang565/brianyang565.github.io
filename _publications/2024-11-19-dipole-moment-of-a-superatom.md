We use homodyne detection to measure the collective atomic dipole moment of an ensemble prepared in a superposition of spatially phased Dicke states having at most two excitations, a so-called superatom. Homodyne detection isolates the contributions to the radiated intensity that depend linearly on the average value of the collective dipole moment operator. Depending on whether the interference between the atoms and the reference field is constructive or destructive, the combined field exhibits super-Poisson or sub-Poisson statistics.

I presented this work in a contributed talk at [APS DAMOP 2024](/talks/2024-damop-interference-effects). The [accepted manuscript](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.133.213601) is also available from APS.
